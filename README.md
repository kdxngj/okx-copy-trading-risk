## Futures copy trading versus spot copy trading

The choice between futures and spot is more important than the trader leaderboard.

### Futures copy trading

Futures copy trading can replicate leveraged long and short positions. That gives you access to strategies that attempt to profit in both rising and falling markets, but it also introduces liquidation risk, funding fees, and faster account drawdowns.

A futures lead trader can be profitable over a short period while still taking risks that are unsuitable for your account. For example, a trader using high leverage may produce an impressive monthly return during a strong market move. The same approach can lose heavily when price moves against the position or when several trades are opened close together.

OKX’s copy-trading FAQ says that copy traders can set a maximum amount per order and a maximum total amount for a particular trader. Once the maximum total amount is reached, new positions will not be copied until existing copied positions close.

The FAQ also states that opening-price protection can prevent a copy trade when the copy trader’s opening price differs from the lead trader’s opening price by more than 0.5%. This can reduce some execution risk, but it also means that not every lead-trader position will necessarily be copied.

### Spot copy trading

Spot copy trading buys and sells the underlying cryptocurrency without futures leverage. There is no futures liquidation mechanism, but that does not mean the strategy is risk-free. A spot asset can still fall substantially, and a lead trader may hold a losing position for a long time.

OKX’s spot copy-trading settings include fixed order amounts, proportional order amounts, maximum total investment, a stop loss for the trader, take-profit settings per order, and stop-loss settings per order.

When a lead trader sells a copied spot asset, the copy trader’s position is generally sold automatically as well. OKX notes that the copy trader’s execution price may differ from the lead trader’s price. A copy trader can also sell earlier manually, but the resulting profit or loss will depend on the copy trader’s own entry and exit prices.

For many beginners, spot copy trading is easier to understand because the position is not exposed to futures liquidation. It is still a crypto trade, though, and “no liquidation” should not be confused with “low risk.”

## How proportional copy trading works

OKX supports proportional copy trading, where your order size is calculated from the lead trader’s order value and a multiplier.

For example, if a lead trader opens a position worth 10,000 USDT and your multiplier is 0.1x, your calculated copy-trade value would be 1,000 USDT. OKX documents a multiplier range of 0.01x to 10x for proportional copy-trading settings.

The basic formula is:

text
Your copy-trade amount = Lead trader order value × Your multiplier


This can be more flexible than using exactly the same fixed amount for every order. However, proportional sizing can also produce unexpectedly large positions if the lead trader’s order sizes vary significantly.

Before copying a trader, check:

- The trader’s average order value
- The largest historical order
- The number of open positions
- The trader’s use of leverage
- The maximum total amount you are willing to allocate
- The minimum order requirements for the market
- Whether the selected multiplier creates a position that is too large for your balance

OKX says the maximum total amount for proportional copy trading can be set between 20 and 30,000 USDT, subject to the product and account conditions shown in the platform.

A small multiplier does not automatically make a strategy safe. If the lead trader opens many positions, the combined exposure can still become large. The maximum total amount is therefore more important than the multiplier alone.

## How to choose an OKX lead trader

A leaderboard is useful for discovery, but it is a poor substitute for risk analysis. A trader at the top of the ranking may have achieved that position through leverage, concentrated positions, or a short period of favorable market conditions.

A more practical review process looks like this:

### 1. Start with the trading product

Decide whether you are reviewing spot traders, futures traders, or bot strategies. Comparing a spot trader’s return directly with a high-leverage futures trader’s return is not meaningful because the risk profiles are different.

### 2. Look beyond total return

Check the trader’s drawdown, consistency, number of trades, average holding period, and current open exposure. A high return with a severe drawdown may be unsuitable if you cannot tolerate similar losses.

### 3. Check how long the record covers

A trader with a short record may simply have benefited from a particular market trend. A longer history provides more context, although it still cannot guarantee future performance.

### 4. Examine position concentration

If most of the trader’s result comes from one token or one large position, the strategy is more fragile than a diversified record might suggest.

### 5. Set your own limits

Use a maximum amount per order and a maximum total amount. Do not allow the lead trader’s settings to determine how much of your account is at risk.

### 6. Monitor changes

A lead trader can change markets, position size, leverage, and trading frequency. Copy trading is automatic execution, not automatic supervision. Review the account periodically and stop copying if the strategy no longer matches your original reason for following it.

## Why copy trades can fail

A copy trade may fail even when the lead trader’s order appears successful. OKX lists several possible reasons, including insufficient funds, minimum order requirements, maximum total amount limits, price differences, and product restrictions.

Common causes include:

### Minimum order amount

If the calculated copy-trade value is below the minimum order value for the selected market, the order may not be placed. This can happen with a small fixed amount or a low proportional multiplier.

### Maximum total amount reached

When your open copied positions reach the maximum total amount you selected, new positions may stop copying until an existing position is closed.

### Price difference

OKX’s slippage protection can cancel a copied trade if the execution price differs too much from the lead trader’s opening price. The documented threshold for one copy-trading setting is 0.5%.

### Lead trader limits

OKX states that a lead trader may be unable to create a copied position if the lead account has less than 500 USDT when opening a trade, if the daily lead-trade limit has been reached, or if the contract position exceeds the relevant limit.

### Insufficient account balance

Your balance must cover the copy order, fees, and any required margin. A lead trader’s account size does not increase the amount available in your account.
